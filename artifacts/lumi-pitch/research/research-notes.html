1. Problem evidence and product fit

Aldakhil et al. (2024) pooled 18 studies with 30,243 participants, estimating dyslexia prevalence of 11% among studied Arab primary-school children aged 6–13 [1]. The authors explicitly call for standardized diagnostics and broader country coverage. Eight studies came from Egypt and four from Saudi Arabia: this is not a representative census of every Arab country. The abstract reports subgroup estimates that do not simply reconstruct the pooled figure; use the overall estimate with its limitations, not an invented country-specific rate.

UNICEF Jordan describes persistent foundational reading and mathematics challenges despite primary enrollment exceeding 97%, and barriers for vulnerable children [2]. UNICEF’s inclusive-education guidance emphasizes accessible materials, teacher capacity, and system change [3]. These sources establish a real support need; neither proves that Lumi improves attainment or that AI is the best intervention.

Lumi’s current code and canonical product guidance support three adult-assigned interaction modes, adult-controlled Arabic/English interfaces, dyslexia voice support, small-step explanations, predictable autism interactions, and adult learning observations. Warm-up content is currently Arabic-only, ages 6–12; English interface support must not be presented as an English standardized assessment. Adult observations are non-diagnostic. Source files reviewed include student chat, AI prompt construction, home/adult entry, and placement rollout guidance. This was read-only product inspection, not a clinical validation or a comprehensive production test.

The deck intentionally does not claim diagnosis, IQ measurement, proven grade improvement, curriculum certification, fully validated safety, homework-upload functionality, or replacement of teachers/specialists. UNESCO recommends age-appropriate pedagogical validation, human oversight and privacy safeguards for generative AI in education [4]. Adult setup alone is not proof of compliance.

4. Business model and go-to-market

Proposed prices: schools/centers $5/student/month billed annually ($60 per seat/year); families $10/child/month ($120 annualized if retained for 12 months). These are founder hypotheses, not booked revenue or validated willingness to pay. Separate annual commitment from monthly active usage, and explain cancellation, sponsored access and unused-seat rules before selling.

Economics to measure: text-model inference + speech recognition + generated speech + storage/hosting + payment costs + onboarding/support per active learner. Contribution margin = recognized subscription revenue minus attributable variable costs. Gross margin, CAC, LTV and payback are currently unknown. Do not assert “unlimited voice” or attractive margins before metering high-usage households and school sessions.

  1. Recruit: use @lumi.mena demonstrations, teacher introductions and specialist referrals to invite consenting adults into a supervised pilot. Instagram views are reach, not unique qualified leads.
  2. Activate: adult setup, language/mode assignment, first supervised child session. Record where setup fails and how much staff time is required.
  3. Evaluate: repeat use, session completion, teacher-rated usefulness, errors, adult support burden, child comfort and opt-outs. Define any learning measures with qualified specialists before collecting them; no causal efficacy claim without a suitable study.
  4. Convert: an explicit paid offer, procurement decision and renewal test. Track conversion denominators by source; do not infer a funnel from 37 signups and 10,000 views collected over different windows.
  5. Expand: referrals and a small second-market pilot only after local fit, safety, reliability and unit economics are demonstrated.

  • The supplied 19,735 learning-difficulty students / 1,070 schools / 4,006 schools figures were not verified in the reviewed material. Search results repeatedly confused Jordan the country with a US school district; those results were rejected. No claim that the figures are false is made.
  • Instagram could not be independently retrieved through the research tool (403). The user-confirmed link is used only as a contact channel; views remain founder-reported.
  • No signed pilot contract, payment evidence, retained-user cohort, learning-outcome evaluation or customer acquisition cost was supplied.
  • The study age band (6–13), product warm-up band (6–12), and primary enrollment population differ. Validate the exact eligible cohort before a fundraising valuation or financial forecast.
